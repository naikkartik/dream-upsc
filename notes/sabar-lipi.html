The Scholar of the Sora People

ସୋରା ଜନଗୋଷ୍ଠୀର ପଣ୍ଡିତ

Mangei Gomango, reverently known as Pandita Sabara ("scholar of the Sora"), was born on 16 June 1916 in Marichaguda in the Jeypore Estate of present-day Odisha. A self-taught polymath, he was not only a language activist but also a poet, an Ayurvedic scholar, and a social reformer who dedicated his life to uplifting the Sora tribal community.

The Sora people (also called Saura, Saora, Savara, or Sabara) are an ancient Austroasiatic Munda community living in the hilly tracts of southern Odisha and northern coastal Andhra Pradesh. Though they had a rich oral tradition, they had no written script of their own — until Gomango changed everything.

In 1936, Gomango established a press at Puthasahi in the Gunupur subdivision, dedicating himself to spreading literacy and preserving Sora culture through the script he had devised. He received the Odisha Sahitya Academy award in recognition of his monumental contribution to Indian literary heritage.

"The temple of Matarabnam in Marichaguda played a great role in the invention of the script. It is believed that the Sorang Sompeng alphabet came to Gomango in a divine vision on 18 June 1936."

Birth of the Visionary

Mangei Gomango is born on 16 June in Marichaguda, Jeypore Estate, Odisha, into the Sora tribal community.

18 June 1936

The Divine Vision / ଦିବ୍ୟ ଦର୍ଶନ

At the Matarabnam temple in Marichaguda, Gomango receives a divine vision revealing the Sorang Sompeng alphabet — a moment that would transform tribal literacy forever.

1936

First Press Established

Gomango establishes a printing press at Puthasahi, Gunupur subdivision, to spread the new script and produce literature in the Sora language.

1966

Formal Development

The Sorang Sompeng script is formally developed and published for wider educational dissemination across Sora communities in Odisha and Andhra Pradesh.

1980

Mangei Gomango passes away, leaving behind an enduring legacy — a living script used by Sora communities to this day.

2012

The Sorang Sompeng script is added to the Unicode Standard (version 6.1), Unicode block U+110D0–U+110FF, giving it global digital recognition.

Present

Activists, scholars, and organisations continue to push for Saura language's inclusion in the 8th Schedule of the Indian Constitution and to develop digital tools for the script.

The Writing System

Sorang Sompeng Script

ସୋରଙ୍ଗ ସୋମ୍ପେଙ୍ଗ ଲିପି

🔤 About the Script

Sorang Sompeng is an alphabetic script written left to right. It was created specifically for the Sora (Munda) language. The name "Sorang Sompeng" means roughly "the letters of the Soras" in the Sora language itself. It has 24 consonants and 6 vowels, plus its own numeral system.

🌐 Usage & Status

The script is used primarily in religious rites of the Matar Banom neo-animist movement and also for secular education and literature. It is spoken by approximately 3 lakh people in Odisha and Andhra Pradesh. Only ~30% can write it fluently, making preservation efforts critical.

Cultural Significance

ସାଂସ୍କୃତିକ ମହତ୍ତ୍ୱ
🛕

Sacred & Spiritual

ଧାର୍ମିକ ଓ ଆଧ୍ୟାତ୍ମିକ

Sabar Lipi was born from a divine vision and is used primarily in the religious rites of the Matar Banom neo-animist movement. It is considered a liturgical language, akin to Sanskrit or Latin in other traditions — the sacred language of Sora ritual.

🏔️

Land & Identity

ଭୂମି ଓ ପରିଚୟ

The Sora people of the Rayagada, Gajapati, and Bargarh hills of Odisha, and coastal Andhra Pradesh, see the script as inseparable from their identity. At least 5 lakh tribals speak Sora — the script is their written soul.

📚

Literary Heritage

ସାହିତ୍ୟ ସଂପଦ

Gomango was not only a script-maker but a poet. His press at Gunupur produced literature in Sora, creating the foundation for a written Sora literary tradition. Odisha Sahitya Academy honoured this contribution with a formal award.

🌍

Global Recognition

ଆନ୍ତର୍ଜାତୀୟ ସ୍ୱୀକୃତି

In 2012, Sorang Sompeng became one of the few tribal scripts in the world to receive Unicode encoding (U+110D0–U+110FF). Microsoft's Nirmala UI font supports it. It is one of only 4 tribal languages in Odisha with a written script.

⚠️

Endangered Status

ବିପଦଗ୍ରସ୍ତ ଅବସ୍ଥା

While all Sora people speak the language, only ~30% can write Sabar Lipi fluently. Preservation efforts — software tools, schools, and advocacy for 8th Schedule inclusion — are urgently needed to prevent this heritage from fading.

🔬

Linguistic Uniqueness

ଭାଷାତାତ୍ତ୍ୱିକ ବୈଶିଷ୍ଟ୍ୟ

Sora is an Austroasiatic Munda language — among the oldest language families in the Indian subcontinent. Sabar Lipi was created independent of all existing scripts, making it a truly original invention, not derived from Brahmi or Devanagari.
